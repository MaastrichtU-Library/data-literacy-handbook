:::{prf:definition}
:class: hint gold
**Data literacy** refers to how competent an individual is in collecting, analysing, interpreting, evaluating, storing, sharing, and communicating data, while bearing ethical, integrity, privacy, and security considerations.
:::

As you might have noticed in the definition above, there are multiple competences and aspects to consider in the journey to become data literate. You will require *knowledge* (e.g., the know what), *skills* (e.g., the know how), but also fundamental *attitudes* (e.g., embracing specific personal and/or institutional values).

## Applying the concept

Consider the following case:

_Robert is a young researcher, who has recently attended a series of trainings by a {term}`data steward` in his research institution. Because of that, it could be assumed that Robert has already acquired the necessary *knowledge* and *skills* to effectively and ethically collect, process, and manage research data. However, when pressured by work responsibilities and deliverable deadlines, Robert could still neglect the proper development of a *Data Management Plan* (DMP) in a specific project, particularly if Robert has not yet embraced the importance of *FAIR principles* (an important *attitude* in data literacy!)._

You could judge Robert's case above, but you could perform an assessment for the same with yourself. For instance, you can quickly assess yourself in a very specific aspect of data literacy by considering the following questions about *data storage* practices:

- Do you understand the importance of version control in one's dataset? [*knowledge*]
- Can you identify suitable storage options for data, according to its characteristics and applicable? [*skills*]
- Do you recognise the importance of a coherent and consistent structure when storing data? [*attitude*]

To develop one's data literacy to its full potential, attention must be paid to these three aspects: *knowledge*, *skills*, and *attitudes*. To understand how the content of this book helps your learning, you will find a list of relevant {term}`data literacy competences` at the beginning of each section.

## Data literacy and related skills

Developing your data literacy will help you become a better researcher. However, there are also other fundamental academic skills you will need when working with data and information, whether physical or digital. Becoming data literate will also require you to develop some [related literacies](../basics/related-literacies-overlap.md), namely _digital_ literacy and _information_ literacy.
