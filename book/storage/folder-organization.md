## Best practices for data organization

## Document your structure 
You already have a structure in your TF folder, but chances are, you have not documented it. It may not seem important to document your structure at this point, as you may "know your folder like the back of your hand". Nonetheless, it a valuable process to:

- assess your file management system;
- ease a new member of the team to your TF structure;
- ease retrieval of information from your TF for all members of the RDM team. 
The easiest way to do this, is through a README file. In there, you can describe your structure as well as your rationale for it. 
Resource: Example of README (from TF6).


## Think about the purpose of each main folder
When creating your structure, it is helpful to think about the purpose of each folder. For example, you can ask yourself:

- is it an active folder? e.g. you will be actively working in the documents that are collected here, by modifying them until you have a final version.
- is it a static folder? e.g. it collects final products and resources that you may use in your TF. 
The answer to these questions can guide you in determining how to structure your folder and sub-folders, as well as your naming convention. 

## Organise folders hierarchically...
Structure often means hierarchy. When it comes to our TF folders, that can also be helpful. Parent folders or main folders contain subfolders which may also contain further subfolders. The result is a tree-like hierarchy that organizes files based on categories. This tree structure can help you find your documents faster.

## ... but recognise when the hierarchy is not serving you anymore
However, make sure that you do not create unnecessary nested levels of folders. As a rule of thumb, three levels of nested-ness in your TF folder should be enough to keep you organised. If you have to access more than three subfolders before finding a document, you may want to reassess your system. Do you really need all these subfolders?

## Archive completed projects/set up an archive
Archive completed projects. When a project is completed, it's important to move it out of the way so that it doesn't clutter up your folder. This is important to have a clear and clutter-free digital environment. You should regularly delete or archive any files that are unnecessary, outdated, or no longer in use. This will also minimise your fatigue once the team is asked to delete unnecessary documents, as the archive will have a collection of documents that you can delete. 

## Be proactive about creating (sub)folders
When you develop your folder structure, you can create a group of folders or subfolder that show how you want your folder to be structured. Assess your needs, find an answer, then re-sort your current documents in different folders. 

## Avoid duplicates
Each document should belong to a specific folder or subfolder. If not possible, then insert hyperlinks, not a duplicate of the document. Why? Having duplicated documents means you need to update the document in two different locations.  
If you have older and newer versions of a document, consider deleting or archiving the older version if it is not only used or useful.  

## Preserve raw data unmodified
Your raw data is your treasure, do not modify it. Keep it unmodified stored separate and work on a copy.

## Regularly assess your file management system
Micro-assessments happen every time you need to put a document in a folder. If you find yourself needing struggling to understand where to place it or, vice-versa, if you find yourself struggling with finding documents within a/your taskforce, you may want to reflect why the current system is not working. 
! Whenever you are assessing your file management system, remember to update your readme if you incur in structural changes. 

## Stick to it
The system you set up is not going to be perfect, but in order to be effective you must commit to it and stick to it, at least for a little bit, before reassessing. 

## Where to start? (Procedure)
Restructuring a folder is quite the task. It is not a 5-minute task, and we advise you to follow this procedure as it gives you small tasks to achieve your goal. Try to complete one step at a time, and one step per "restructuring session". 
Tip: Spread the restructuring task over a few days or weeks; if you want to tackle it in one go, do not forget to take breaks! 
Note: The following steps guide you through a whole restructuring of your folder. If you do not believe that is necessary, you can "just" write a clear and complete README file. 

🧭 Step 1: Create your level1 structure.
Lay the foundation of your new structure. For this, think about the way you work in your TF. Think about the general rules displayed above and think about how you work in the taskforce. Which kind of big areas of work can you individuate? 
In practice: 

- Create these folders as empty folders, do not just rename the folders you already have
- We strongly recommend having a "Management" folder in which you can put your taskforce profile document, etc. 
- Create a "Temporary sort" folder 
- Create an "Archive" folder
- Create a README file (or copy ours) and start editing it

🧹 Step 2: Move EVERYTHING in the "Temporary sort" folder.
Move all of your old/original folders, files, etc. that are now at the level 1 to your "Temporary sort" folder. 
At this point, you should have, on level1, only your new folder structure and your README file.

🏗️ Step 3: Think about your level 2 and level 3 structure.
At this point, you have your new areas, and your folder looks exactly how you will want it at the end, for level one. This is the time to start thinking about the next levels. You may already have created some subfolders, that is great. Refine your level 2 and 3. Go into each area folder you have created and think about the subfolders that make sense for you there and create them. 
Try to be as extensive as possible. Do not only think in terms of the documents and files that are now in your "Temporary sort", think instead in terms of which documents you may also have in the future. Creating the structure now will reduce your fatigue later as you will only have to sort your documents and not also think about whether to create a new folder. 
Do not forget to update your README file accordingly!

You may want to still rename a few files and give one last check to your README file to make sure that it is documenting your final structure. But all in all, congrats, at this point you have a new folder structure! Remember to stick to it and keep your README file updated.
